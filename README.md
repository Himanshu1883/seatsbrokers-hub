# SeatsBrokers Hub

SeatsBrokers — Landing Page Rebuild Brief

(Master prompt — hand this to a designer, developer, or AI website builder)

1. PROJECT SUMMARY

Rebuild the SeatsBrokers homepage/landing page from scratch. SeatsBrokers is a B2B live-event ticket distribution and marketplace intelligence platform serving two core partner types:

Seller Partners — ticket brokers/resellers who want to list and move inventory across every major marketplace (Ticketmaster, StubHub, Vivid Seats, SeatGeek, AXS, Viagogo, TickPick, Gametime, etc.) without manual busywork.

Travel Partners — travel agencies, tour operators, concierge desks, and OTAs who want to bundle verified event tickets into itineraries and packages for their clients.

The new site should feel like a modern, techy, data-driven B2B SaaS platform — closer to a fintech/infrastructure product than a consumer ticket shop — while still being visually rooted in live sports and entertainment (stadiums, crowds, trophies, teams). Think "Stripe meets StubHub's back office."

This is a from-scratch redesign, not a reskin. Use the old site and the four reference platforms below as source material — pull structure, features, and credibility points from each, but rewrite everything into a single, cohesive, original voice. Nothing should read as copy-pasted from any one source.

2. BRAND IDENTITY (LOCKED)

Logo: Existing SeatsBrokers wordmark + arrow/chevron seat mark (provided asset). Keep the mark; the color treatment updates per the palette below.

Primary color: #198754 — deep trust-green. Used for: CTA buttons, links, active nav states, icon strokes/fills, section accent lines, chart/data highlight color, hover states.

Background: Clean white / off-white (#FFFFFF, #F8F9FA for alternating sections). Minimal, uncluttered, generous white space — this is a premium B2B tool, not a busy marketplace page.

Text: Near-black neutral (#1A1A1A or #212529) for body copy; #198754 reserved for emphasis words, stat numbers, and interactive elements — don't overuse it or it stops feeling premium.

Supporting neutrals: Light gray borders/dividers (#E9ECEF), muted gray secondary text (#6C757D).

3. TYPOGRAPHY

Headings: A geometric, techy sans-serif — Space Grotesk, Sora, or General Sans. Bold weights for hero/section headlines to give the "infrastructure/data platform" feel.

Body: Inter or IBM Plex Sans — clean, highly legible, neutral.

Data/stat accents: A monospace touch — JetBrains Mono or Space Mono — used sparingly for big stat numbers (e.g. "2,000,000+", "30+ YEARS", "165 COUNTRIES") to reinforce the live-data, dashboard feel.

4. IMAGERY DIRECTION

Full-bleed, high-resolution banner photography (Unsplash-sourced) — not stock-clipart, real editorial-quality shots:

Packed football/soccer stadiums at night, crowd shots with lights

Championship trophies / trophy lift moments

Concert crowds and arena lighting

Team celebration moments

Empty stadium seating (great for texture/section backgrounds)

Hero and major section banners use a dark gradient overlay (black/green-tinted, ~40–60% opacity) so white/green text stays legible over the photo.

Avoid generic "handshake" or "laptop on desk" B2B stock photography — the emotional hook here is live sport and entertainment, the tech is the engine underneath it.

5. MOTION / "TECHY FEEL"

Hero headline uses an auto-typing (typewriter) effect, cycling through a rotating set of words/phrases, e.g.:

"Powering ticket distribution for Football. Concerts. Championships. Every Seat."

Reuse the typing effect in at least one more section — e.g., a tagline strip above the stats bar, or inside the "How It Works" intro, cycling through partner types: "Built for Brokers. Travel Agencies. Rights Holders."

Subtle scroll-triggered fade/slide-ins for section reveals, animated counters on the stats bar (numbers count up on scroll into view), and a soft hover-lift on all cards/buttons.

Keep motion restrained and purposeful — techy ≠ busy. Every animation should feel like it's signaling "live system," not decoration.

6. SITE STRUCTURE

Navbar: Logo | About Us | Seller Partners | Travel Partners | (Affiliates — optional secondary) | Contact | Login (secondary button) | Get Started (primary green CTA button)

Footer: Logo + tagline, product/partner links, social, contact/offices, legal links, newsletter signup ("Stay in the loop — affiliate updates & seasonal promo packs").

7. HOMEPAGE SECTION-BY-SECTION BUILD

A. Hero Section

Full-bleed stadium/crowd banner image, dark gradient overlay.

Auto-typing animated headline (see Section 5).

Sub-headline: one or two lines establishing SeatsBrokers as a trusted, 30+ year B2B distribution platform for global live-event inventory.

Dual CTA buttons: "Become a Seller Partner" (primary, green) / "Become a Travel Partner" (secondary, outline).

Optional: small trust strip directly under the CTA — "30+ Years in Ticketing · 10K+ Partners · 3 Global Offices" — as an immediate credibility anchor (pulled from old site, kept as-is since it's a genuine differentiator none of the references have).

B. Marketplace / Trust Logo Wall

Horizontal scrolling or grid strip of marketplace logos your inventory reaches: Ticketmaster, StubHub, Vivid Seats, SeatGeek, AXS, Viagogo, TickPick, Gametime, TicketNetwork, etc.

Framed as "Your inventory, everywhere fans buy" — merges Lysted's "one listing, every marketplace" idea with Automatiq's distribution promise.

C. Two-Track Audience Section (core differentiator, expanded from old site)

Two large side-by-side (or stacked-on-mobile) cards, each with its own banner image:

Seller Partners card

Framing: autopilot inventory distribution, real-time dynamic pricing, transparent payouts — a broker never has to babysit dashboards or refresh marketplaces manually (echo Automatiq's Sync/Uptick framing, in original words).

Tags: Autopilot Workflows · Real-Time Pricing · Zero-Fee Listing · Transparent Payouts

Travel Partners card

D. "How It Works" (3-step, from old site, kept — it's clean and works)

Access Inventory — one platform, global curated event inventory

Integrate Seamlessly — API or white-label integration into your systems

Distribute Globally — sell through your own channels, packages, or storefronts

E. Live Market Intelligence Section (new — inspired by ProTickets + Automatiq DataIQ/Uptick)

This is the section that gives the site its "techy platform" identity — none of this existed on the old site, but it should now, since it's the credibility layer every reference site leans on hardest.

Visual: dashboard mockup/screenshot placeholder showing live inventory, pricing trends, or a market heatmap.

Copy: real-time dynamic pricing, live inventory sync across marketplaces, automated order routing, market-level analytics for smarter broker decisions.

Could use 3–4 feature tiles: Smart Pricing Engine, Real-Time Inventory Sync, Market Analytics Dashboard, Automated Order Routing.

F. Seller Tools Grid (from old seller.html, expanded visually)

H. Global Reach / Network Section (from old site's "Global Distribution" block)

Copy: bridges live inventory with a worldwide network of travel companies, concierge services, corporate partners, offline agents, and resellers.

Optional: world map graphic or stat-driven visual (countries covered, cities served).

Reinforce: Global Reach · Scalable Model · Flexible Integration · B2B-Focused

I. Stats / Trust Bar (merge old-site stats with reference-style "live data" framing)

Animated count-up numbers on scroll:

2M+ Tickets Delivered

10K+ Partner Network

3 Global Offices

4.7/5 Average Partner Rating (Optionally add a "platforms integrated" or "countries covered" stat if you want to lean further into the ProTickets-style "coverage" flex — keep only if accurate to your real network.)

J. Testimonials

Short, review-style quotes from sellers and travel partners (style borrowed from Automatiq/Lysted's star-review wall) — keep it light, credible, not overproduced.

K. Final CTA Banner

Full-width banner image (trophy lift or packed stadium), dark overlay, centered headline + dual CTA buttons repeating Seller/Travel Partner sign-up — mirrors the hero to close the loop.

L. Footer

Logo, nav links, partner program links, contact/offices, social icons, newsletter signup, legal links, copyright.

8. COPY TONE

Confident, plain-spoken, B2B-operator voice — not hypey, not overly corporate. Lead with outcomes ("stop babysitting spreadsheets," "sell everywhere without lifting a finger," "protect your travelers from unreliable sources") rather than vague buzzwords. Every section should answer "what does this actually do for me" within the first sentence.

9. TECHNICAL NOTES

Fully responsive (mobile-first breakpoints), fast-loading despite heavy imagery (lazy-load below-the-fold banners).

Section backgrounds alternate white / off-white / dark-photo-banner to create rhythm down the page.

All CTAs route to the correct existing flows: Seller signup, Travel Partner signup, Login.

Reuse the same visual system (color, type, imagery style, motion) across the future Seller Partners and Travel Partners subpages so the whole site feels like one product.

Sources synthesized into this brief: Automatiq.com (audience segmentation, product-suite structure, trust stats), Tixstock.com (B2B distribution/infrastructure positioning), Lysted.com (one-listing-everywhere simplicity, consumer-friendly tone), ProTickets.ca (techy dashboard feature-block layout, live data/market intelligence framing), and the existing SeatsBrokers.com (Seller/Travel Partner dual-track structure, 30+ year trust stats, tools grids, global distribution model) — plus your locked brand system (logo, #198754, typography, imagery, and typing-effect motion). build it add animation in each section on scroll down or up make the sticky sections too
